Road-weather sensor logs from winter drives at Mcity (MARWIS, part of MCity-WSPI~\citep{wspi}, CC-BY-4.0) give an optical grip proxy rather than tire friction, so we compare ratios to dry. Median grip relative to dry (95\% cluster-bootstrap CI over drives): dry 1.00 [1.00, 1.00] ($N=147$ samples, 1 drive); snow 0.30 [0.30, 0.35] ($N=2,248$ samples, 3 drives); snow/ice 0.31 [0.30, 0.34] ($N=1,436$ samples, 3 drives). The simulator's snow range spans 0.20--0.41 of dry and its ice range 0.08--0.18: no Mcity reading is that low, so our ice is harsher than these logs (the lowest 1\% of the unlabelled Kalamazoo winter readings, $N=1,972$, are below 0.23 of dry). The reported value changes only every 10\,s or so (median) and averages over a road patch, so the sensor cannot resolve how sharp a transition is at the tire; the ramp lengths of the scenarios are not checked against it.

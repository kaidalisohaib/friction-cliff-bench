Table~\ref{tab:compute} gives the latency of one MPPI control step for one car (median of N = 30 timed repetitions after a warm-up, [2.5, 97.5] percentile range; NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 Laptop GPU, Warp 1.18.0, the GPU was otherwise idle). The latency hardly grows with $K$ because each sample's 150-step rollout runs sequentially in one thread, so the horizon, not the sample count, sets the floor. Batched over 64 cars ($K = 1,024$, $H = 150$), one control step takes fixed mode 58.9 [56.2, 64.1]\,ms (Warp) vs 46.8 [42.7, 50.7]\,ms (C++/CUDA); field mode 92.3 [87.5, 98.5]\,ms (Warp) vs 67.6 [63.5, 71.9]\,ms (C++/CUDA). The simulator alone runs at $2.70\times10^{8}$ control steps/s with 65,536 cars, $2.62\times10^{8}$ control steps/s with 262,144 cars (fixed surfaces). These are engineering measurements, not evaluation results.
